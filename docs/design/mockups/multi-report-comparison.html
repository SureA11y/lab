Comparing many reports, not just two

The Compare tab answers a question about two files. This is what it answers about a whole series — written to be read by whoever asks “are we getting better?”, not only by the person who runs the scanner. Everything below is the real component, rendered by the real engine.

  • 4 variants side by side

What you are looking at

Six nightly runs of one banking scenario over ten weeks, in one view. Rendered from mock payloads in the shape the app exports — rule ids, titles, severities and WCAG references are the scanner's own vocabulary, the counts are invented to tell a legible story. The page answers in this order: what state are we in, what changed, what do we do, and only then the evidence. Someone who stops reading after the first screen should still be right about the site.

What this says

The window is not a smooth line. Failures dropped by 20 between Jun 30 and Jul 14, then gave 6 back between Jul 14 and Jul 28.

One problem comes and goes across the window (hidden content still reachable by keyboard). Comparing any two runs would have called it either fixed or new, depending on which two runs were loaded.

Across the window 53 failing instances were cleared and 17 were introduced. The net figure above is the difference between those two, so a quiet window is not necessarily an idle one.

The longest-standing problem still open has been failing without a break since Jun 2, the first run in the window, so possibly longer than that. Age is the argument for it, not size: it has survived every run since.

1 rule stopped failing but now needs a human to confirm what happened (form fields without a label). That is not the same as fixed, and a failure count on its own cannot tell the two apart.

Jul 14 did not complete cleanly: 2 scans skipped: scan-6, scan-7. A run that covered less had less to find, so its lower count is not an improvement.

Pages analyzed changed across the window (7 → 7 → 7 → 7 → 7 → 9), so the absolute counts are context, not verdict; read the direction from the rule categories.

Why this beats loading two files

“Hidden content still reachable by keyboard” has been fixed and broken again across these six runs. Load Jun 30 against Jul 14 and the tool says it is fixed. Load Jul 14 against Jul 28 and it says it is a new regression. Both readings are wrong, and neither two-run comparison can tell you so — the pattern only exists once you look at more than two runs.

The same is true of the headline: the site is much better than in June, and the Jul 28 run gave instances back. A pair shows one of those facts at a time, and never shows which run is the best the site has ever been.

How it stays honest

The latest run scanned more pages than the earlier ones. Absolute counts are not comparable across a workload change, so the caveat sits in the headline rather than in a footnote, and the verdict is read from the rule categories instead.

Nothing is encoded by colour alone: severity and direction are written in words, every heat cell prints its number, and every chart has a table of the same figures underneath it.

What you are looking at

One release, exported four times filtered by scan tag — desktop, tablet, mobile and high contrast. Nothing is ordered in time, so there is no “improved” or “regressed”: the question is what fails everywhere versus what fails in one place only.

What changes as the series grows

  • Up to ~12 columns — everything renders as in the first view.
  • 12 to ~24 — the time axis samples its labels, heat cells narrow, and the grid scrolls sideways under a pinned first column. Numbers stay printed.
  • Beyond that — the tab holds 24 at once and says so. For a year of nightlies the answer is selection, not more columns: a window (last 10 / last 30), or one column per week keeping that week's worst run. Both happen before the diff, so the engine is untouched.

Cost is linear: one ingest per report, no pairwise explosion. Thirty reports is thirty passes, not four hundred and thirty-five comparisons.

What this is made of

Shipped, and rendered above by the same code the app runs. This page is built by docs/design/mockups/multi-report/build-mockup.js, which imports the renderer and extracts the stylesheet from the app rather than keeping copies — so it cannot drift from what the tab shows.
